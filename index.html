What to ask before you submit

Moneysmart's home loan guidance supports comparing more than the interest rate alone, and that makes the pre-application conversation more useful. Ask which lenders are being compared, what features matter for your situation, whether there are ongoing fees, and how flexible the loan is if your plans change. For investment property borrowers, Moneysmart also flags the need to understand the costs and risks of the strategy before relying on future growth or tax outcomes.

The source page also gives a realistic process frame, a quick initial discussion, lender comparison, then selection and settlement, with many approvals taking 2-4 weeks. That is useful as a working expectation, not a guarantee. A practical broker conversation should therefore cover your documents, any time pressure on the contract, and whether your scenario is standard or likely to need a specialist lender.

How to compare options without repeating the same mistake

Borrowers often compare only headline rate, then discover later that the product fit was wrong. The source page is stronger when read as a decision path, first define the borrower scenario, then compare lenders suited to that scenario, then weigh the product features against the reason for borrowing. That is especially relevant for investors considering interest-only options and for self-employed applicants whose income evidence may not suit a narrow credit policy.

The page also says brokers are legally required to act in the client's best interests, and that individual providers operate under an Australian Credit Licence, are regulated by ASIC, hold AFCA membership and maintain professional indemnity insurance. Those are not reasons to skip your own questions. They are reasons to ask for a clear explanation of why a particular shortlist fits your budget, property plans and timing better than the obvious major bank alternative.

  1. Define the scenario. State whether the loan is for a first home, refinance, investment, construction or self-employed application before comparing lenders.
  2. Prepare the key documents. Bring the income, savings, liabilities and property details that will decide whether a standard or specialist lender is realistic.
  3. Compare the shortlist. Ask why each shortlisted lender suits your file, not only what rate is available.
  4. Test the timeline. Check whether your contract or build schedule fits the stated 2-4 week approval window for many applications.
  5. Confirm the settlement path. Before lodging, make sure the next steps, paperwork responsibility and likely sticking points are clear.
